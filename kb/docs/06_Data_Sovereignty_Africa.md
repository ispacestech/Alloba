# African Data Sovereignty

Operating notes on keeping data and processing aligned with African digital
sovereignty principles.

## Guiding principle

Where the platform processes data of African businesses and consumers, the
default should be to store, process and govern that data **in Africa**, unless a
documented reason says otherwise. Sovereignty is not isolation: it is the
capacity to control, protect and benefit from one's own data.

## Local data protection frameworks

Relevant frameworks include:

- AU Malabo Convention on Cyber Security and Personal Data Protection (2014).
- National laws, e.g. Nigeria Data Protection Act, Kenya Data Protection Act,
  South Africa POPIA, Ghana Data Protection Act.
- Regional economic community instruments.

## Alignment with the ECP `sovereignty` concept

The knowledge engine tags documents related to local processing, cross-border
transfers and storage location. Compliance checks about "where is my data kept"
must be answered from these sources and cite them.

## Implementation guidance

1. Prefer African cloud regions (when available and compliant) for hosting.
2. Document any cross-border transfer and its legal basis (adequacy, SCCs,
   derogations).
3. Offer local processing for sensitive datasets.
4. Involve local counsel for each market the platform serves.
5. Review the hosting and transfer map at least annually.

## Trade-off note

Sovereignty decisions are business trade-offs (cost, latency, resilience) as
well as legal ones. Every decision is recorded as an ADR in the collaboration
system so it can be revisited.
